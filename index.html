Investment conclusion

The power stack is the highest-conviction cluster. AI compute demand remains exceptional, but the scarcer—and often more durable—profit pools are migrating outward into power generation, grid connections, transformers, switchgear and thermal management. The IEA expects data-centre electricity use to roughly double from 485 TWh in 2025 to 950 TWh in 2030, while more than 2,500 GW of generation, storage and large-load projects already sit in grid queues. Annual grid investment needs to rise about 50% from roughly $400 billion by 2030. IEA: Energy & AI · IEA: Grids
Revised central hypothesis
As AI increases compute, electricity and data intensity, excess returns should migrate toward indispensable bottlenecks whose supply expands slowly, whose products are difficult to substitute, and whose scarcity converts into backlog, margins, free cash flow and upward earnings revisions.

This replaces the looser “software to physical assets” rotation narrative. The investable variable is not whether a company is physical or digital; it is whether scarcity is captured economically before valuation discounts the full benefit.

Best risk-adjusted research basket

NVDA, AVGO, VST, NVT, ASML, MU, VRT, MOD, CCJ, ISRG, WMS.

These combine direct exposure, supportive analyst consensus and a credible estimate-revision path. This is a diligence shortlist, not a portfolio or entry instruction.

Highest asymmetric watchlist

KTOS, MP, TEM, SYM, UEC.

Raw upside is large, but so are execution, funding, reimbursement, customer-concentration or project-development risks. Require proof before sizing.

Where the market may be too optimistic

AI server assemblers, mature automation, and select copper producers.

Low margins, customer concentration, cyclical order patterns or already-full multiples reduce the value of thematic exposure.

Analyst target upside is not expected return. It is a time-stamped consensus gap that may widen after a price fall, may use optimistic out-year earnings, and often ignores dilution, cycle risk or multiple compression. The report therefore separates “raw upside” from “quality-adjusted upside.”

Where economic leverage sits

The revised framework treats the future economy as a dependency chain rather than a collection of unrelated sectors. Moving right generally increases optionality and competitive churn; moving left generally increases indispensability but can introduce regulation and capital intensity.

1 · Power & gridGeneration, transmission, transformers, switchgear and thermal infrastructure
2 · ComputeAccelerators, custom silicon, foundry capacity and design tools
3 · Memory & storageHBM, DRAM, flash, HDD and enterprise storage
4 · NetworkingSwitching, optics, connectors, cabling and high-speed manufacturing
5 · PlatformsEnterprise, industrial and vertical AI distribution
6 · Physical AIAutomation, robotics, logistics and autonomy
7 · End marketsHealthcare, defence, space, water and consumer applications

Three-layer portfolio architecture

Tier A · bottlenecks

Indispensable infrastructure

Power/grid, compute, cooling, memory and networking.

Best combination of necessity, exposure proof and near-term earnings measurability. Primary risk is correlated hyperscaler capex and expectations-heavy valuation.

Tier B · adoption

Structural beneficiaries

Defence, critical minerals, healthcare AI, automation and water.

Attractive duration, but outcomes depend more heavily on procurement, regulation, reimbursement, commodity prices or customer adoption.

Tier C · optionality

Emerging applications

Space, consumer AI, emerging software and humanoid robotics.

Potentially exceptional winners, but higher imitation, funding, commercialization and valuation risk. Smaller initial sizing and a higher proof threshold are appropriate.

Do not confuse the two clocks

12-month stock clock

Analyst targets, earnings revisions, order conversion, entry valuation and the next two to four reporting cycles.

Question: Is there enough unpriced earnings improvement to compensate for downside over the next year?

2030–2035 structural clock

Capacity constraints, industry structure, supply response, regulation, substitution and long-run returns on invested capital.

Question: Will this company still control an indispensable bottleneck after competitors and customers respond?

What the Future Economy review changes

The roadmap does not overturn the thesis. It improves the research architecture by separating memory/storage and networking, adding construction/engineering adjacencies, and identifying higher-optionality space and software lanes that should not be mixed with core bottlenecks.

Memory becomes standalone

HBM supply, DRAM pricing, NAND recovery and enterprise storage have different cycles from semiconductor equipment. MU remains the preferred immediate candidate; SNDK becomes an expectations-heavy extension candidate.

Networking becomes standalone

Cluster performance increasingly depends on switching, optics, connectors and manufacturing. AVGO/ANET remain core; APH, FN and CLS expand the diligence queue.

Engineering becomes explicit

AI capacity must be installed, commissioned and maintained. FIX and EME offer cash-generative construction exposure; POWL offers higher electrical upside with greater cyclicality.

Cross-theme portfolio risks

One capex cycle in disguise

AI chips, power, cooling, electrical gear and semicap are correlated through hyperscaler capital spending. Owning all five is diversification by ticker, not necessarily by economic driver.

Valuation and duration

Many leaders trade on out-year earnings. Higher real rates or slower backlog conversion can compress multiples even if end-demand remains intact.

Policy and geopolitics

Semiconductor export controls, defence procurement, nuclear licensing, mineral subsidies and infrastructure funding can change addressable markets or timing.

Supply response

Scarcity rents invite capacity. Transformer, cooling, HBM and power-equipment margins can normalize once new plants qualify and utilization rises.

Execution concentration

High-upside names often depend on one mine, one programme, one customer, one reimbursement code or one production ramp.

Theme ≠ stock

A strong market does not guarantee attractive equity returns. The entry price, earnings revisions, balance sheet and dilution path determine the stock outcome.

Suggested next diligence order: (1) rebuild revenue and margin bridges for MU, VST, NVT, VRT and NVDA; (2) back-solve what growth their current prices require; (3) create bear/base/bull valuation ranges; (4) add dated prove/kill milestones; and only then decide entry levels or position size.

Methodology and evidence posture

Screen construction

  1. Translated the user’s macro playbook and Future Economy Research Roadmap into 12 thematic pathways: memory/storage was separated from semiconductor equipment, networking/interconnect became standalone, and engineering/construction was added as a cross-theme execution lens.
  2. Screened a 77-name liquid US-listed / ADR universe across structural bottlenecks, adoption beneficiaries and optionality.
  3. Retrieved a dated research snapshot of prices, analyst consensus and average 12-month targets. A separate TradingView widget supplies current or exchange-delayed market prices without altering the historical screen.
  4. Excluded or penalized thin target gaps, neutral / negative consensus, weak exposure attribution and extreme execution dependence.
  5. Ranked the survivors by theme durability, earnings visibility, estimate-revision potential, valuation risk and falsifiability.

Important limitations

  • Consensus targets are point-in-time market data, not independently modelled fair values.
  • Coverage breadth differs by stock. Snapshot scores are screening aids, not valuation models, and do not update automatically.
  • Trailing P/E is not comparable across cyclical, pre-profit and recently restructured companies.
  • The screen is global-thematic but US-listing-biased; several European and Asian leaders are underrepresented.
  • No portfolio constraints, tax position, base currency or risk budget were provided.
